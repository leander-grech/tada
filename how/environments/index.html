    Episodes and temporal windowing¶

    Summary

    An episode is one stream of 20 arrivals to one airport. The policy does not see the whole stream: temporal windowing shows it the next 10 flights in the AMAN sequence, in 10 fixed slots. When a flight at the head of the queue lands or is released, the flights behind it move up and the next flight in the queue takes the last slot. Nothing the policy observes, and nothing its critic has to predict, depends on how long the stream is, so the same policy can be run on longer streams. The environment keeps two copies of the traffic: the live world, where clearances are executed, and the predicted world, a do-nothing forecast of it.

    Source: single-agent/atc_env/windowed_env.py (class WindowedATCEnv), single-agent/config/windowed_config.py, and the base class in single-agent/atc_env/single_agent_env.py.

    One episode¶

    An episode starts from a generated scenario: 20 flights bound for one airport, each with an AMAN target landing time. The flights enter the terminal area one after another, the policy issues clearances, and the episode runs until every flight has landed or the episode fails.

    The policy acts once every 45 s of simulated time (Config.TIME_BETWEEN_ACTIONS). At each decision it picks one aircraft and one clearance for it, and may ask for a second pick before the clock moves (reselection). The environment then applies the clearances, advances the simulation, and returns a new observation and a reward.

    Live world and predicted world¶

    The simulation runs at two levels.

    The live world is the traffic as it actually develops. It is integrated in 1 s steps, and clearances are applied to it at the 45 s decision points. Everything that counts as an outcome is read from the live world: landings and their time deviations, conflicts, and aircraft leaving the airspace.

    The predicted world is a copy of the live world that is simulated forward with no further clearances. This no-operation rollout shows what will happen if the policy does nothing from now on. It is re-run after every transition, and it provides:

    • the predicted landing time of every flight, and so its predicted time deviation;
    • every predicted proximity event between two aircraft, with its time.

    These predictions feed the observation, the shaping terms of the reward, the release rule below and one of the action masks. The predicted world never ends an episode and never decides whether an episode is solved.

    The rollout has a receding horizon. It covers 2880 s, or the time until the last flight in the window is due if that is longer, plus a margin of 20 decision steps (900 s) for late flights, and never more than 200 steps. Its length therefore depends on the window and not on the stream. Two details of how landing times are read from it:

    • the simulator reports a landing as mistimed only beyond a tolerance of 10 s, so a flight that the rollout lands within 10 s of its target is predicted exactly on target;
    • a flight that the rollout does not land is placed at the rollout's end, meaning "at least that late".

    During reselection the rollout is repeated with the queued clearance applied, so the second pick sees the predicted effect of the first.

    The window¶

    Who is in the window¶

    All flights that have not landed and have not been released form a queue, ordered by AMAN target landing time. The window is the first 10 of them.

    rule
    membership the next 10 flights in AMAN order that have neither landed nor been released, whether or not they have entered the terminal area
    slots slot 1 is the head of the queue; slot order is queue order
    a flight lands or is released it leaves the window; the flights behind it shift up one slot and the next flight in the queue enters the last slot
    end of the stream when fewer than 10 flights remain, the trailing slots are empty and masked

    The slot index carries no meaning beyond the order: the policy network applies the same encoders to every slot.

    Pending flights¶

    A flight can hold a slot before it has spawned. Such a pending flight is not yet under control. It is visible to the policy and contributes its schedule and its route, so the policy can plan around it, but it cannot receive a clearance:

    • its row of the clearance mask allows only DO_NOTHING;
    • the aircraft-selection mask offers only flights under control whenever at least one exists.

    The policy therefore acts only on flights that are both inside the window and under control.

    Release¶

    A flight does not have to stay in the window until it touches down. Once its landing is settled it is released: it leaves the window, receives no further clearances, and keeps flying to the runway in the live world. This frees its slot for a flight that still needs attention.

    Release works from the head of the queue. After each 45 s step the environment walks down the queue and releases every flight that is locked, stopping at the first one that is not. A flight is locked when all of the following hold:

    • it is under control;
    • the predicted world lands it within the rollout;
    • its predicted landing is within 30 s of its AMAN target (release_dev_s);
    • it is not part of any predicted proximity event.

    A released flight is still part of the episode. The flights behind it continue to see it through their landing-gap and conflict features, a conflict that involves it still ends the episode, and its actual landing still counts towards whether the episode is solved. Its landing reward is paid at release from the predicted deviation and corrected at touchdown if the outcome differs (Objective and reward).

    At the tail of a stream every airborne flight may already be released. The window then shows those flights again, with only DO_NOTHING legal, so that there is always a slot to pick.

    Release is switched on per environment (config key release_locked, with release_dev_s and pay_at_release). Both published policies were trained with it, and the evaluation tools read these keys from each model's run_meta.json so that a policy is always run in the environment it was trained in.

    What the window hides¶

    The price of a fixed window is explicit partial observability: a flight that is under control but more than ten places behind the head of the queue is not represented. The environment counts the decision steps on which this happens and reports the fraction in the episode metrics as window_overflow_frac.

    Stream-stationary observables¶

    The design rule of the windowed environment is that nothing the policy observes, and nothing its critic has to predict, may depend on how many flights the stream has. A 20-flight scenario is treated as a finite excerpt of an endless arrival stream. Five things follow from the rule:

    • The forecast covers the window, not the scenario (the receding horizon above).
    • Time features are relative. Each flight carries its scheduled time to its target, and the global time feature is the time until the last flight in the window is due. Absolute scenario time does not appear.
    • Counts and maxima are taken over the window. The global count is the number of window flights under control, not the number of occupied slots, which would only fall as the stream ran out. The global conflict feature is the maximum over the flights in the window.
    • The reward is paid per flight, once, and the penalty for a conflict is a constant. Neither scales with the number of flights left.
    • The end of the stream is not a terminal state (next section).

    The observation features themselves are described on Observations.

    Termination and truncation¶

    event in the live world signal end_reason
    a conflict: two aircraft within 3 NM and 500 ft terminated separation
    an aircraft leaves the airspace terminated airspace_exit
    every flight has landed truncated success if every flight is within 60 s of its target, otherwise delayed
    the step limit is reached truncated timeout

    A conflict or an airspace exit is a true termination. The episode stops at once and the reward carries a fixed penalty. Only realised events count: a predicted conflict never terminates an episode (Separation rules).

    The stream running dry is reported as a truncation, not a termination. In a continuing stream more flights would follow, and the critic sees only the window, not how many flights remain. PPO therefore bootstraps from the critic's value at that point instead of treating it as an absorbing end. An episode with end_reason = "success" is a solved episode.

    The step limit is sized per scenario: the number of 45 s steps to the last target landing time, plus 6, and at least 48. Training caps it at 200 steps per 20-flight segment. The evaluation scripts lift the cap, so every stream runs to its own horizon.

    A zero-time reselection pick cannot end an episode, because nothing moves.

    The scenario for each procedure¶

    One environment class serves both procedures. The procedure is a property of the scenario, chosen by the use case:

    TMB PMS
    airport, procedure Milan Malpensa, trombone Milan Bergamo, point merge
    use case 1 (MissionDifficulty.VALIDATION_USE_CASE_1, the default) 2 (MissionDifficulty.VALIDATION_USE_CASE_2)
    generator settings single-agent/config/simulation_config_mxp/ single-agent/config/simulation_config_bgy/
    clearance set (TADA_ACTION_SET) v2 v2pms
    routes at the start of an episode cut to the short AMAN route; trombone levels stored for lengthen the whole sequencing leg kept; shortened by direct to merge point

    The use case is selected in three places:

    • the environment config key use_case (1 or 2), recorded in the model's run_meta.json;
    • python main_jax.py --use-case 1|2 when training;
    • python -m evaluation.run_search --procedure tmb|pms, or --use-case on analysis.score_windowed, when evaluating.

    With TADA_GENERATOR=procedure (the default) the scenario generator reads the settings directory that belongs to the use case, and the first scenario that a seed draws is used: there is no rejection filter. The clearance set has to match the procedure: v2pms refuses a route without a merge point, and loading a model under a clearance set other than the one in its run_meta.json stops with an error. The clearance sets are described on Actions and reselection.

    Longer and stitched streams¶

    Because of the design rule above, stream length is only a setting of the environment (scenario_aircraft_count). Training uses this for its curriculum, which starts on 10-flight streams and then moves to 20 (Train a policy). The published policies were evaluated on single 20-flight streams. The public code also supports two kinds of longer stream.

    A longer generated stream. --flights 40 on analysis.score_windowed generates 40 flights in one sequence.

    A stitched stream. A stream is built from several independently generated 20-flight segments (Simulator._stitched_scenario in single-agent/simulator/simulator.py):

    • the first target landing of each further segment comes a random cooling gap after the last target landing of the previous one, drawn uniformly from 120 to 900 s by default;
    • the spawn times and targets of a segment shift together, so its internal spacing is untouched;
    • callsigns and stored trombone sections are renumbered to continue the queue.

    For a fixed seed the segments and the gaps are deterministic. With stitching, the step limit grows with the number of segments, and the cost of a clearance is divided by it, so that the priority order of the objectives is kept on the longer stream.

    option
    training python main_jax.py --stitch-segments 2 --stitch-gap 120 900
    scoring python -m analysis.score_windowed --segments 2 --gap 120 900
    rendering python render_policy.py --env windowed --segments 2

    analysis/long_stream_seeds.py screens seeds for stitched streams that are feasible: by default, streams in which no flight is predicted to land more than 650 s early when no clearance is given. The experiments with long streams during development are in the Research notes.

    Configuration¶

    Defaults from single-agent/config/windowed_config.py (WindowedConfig) and single-agent/config/config.py (Config):

    setting value meaning
    SCENARIO_AIRCRAFT_COUNT 20 flights per scenario
    WINDOW_SIZE 10 slots; equal to Config.N_AIRCRAFT_MAX
    Config.TIME_BETWEEN_ACTIONS 45 s decision interval
    DIFFICULTY VALIDATION_USE_CASE_1 default scenario: the Malpensa trombone
    WINDOW_UNDER_CONTROL_ONLY False pending flights hold their place in the window
    PENDING_ACTIONABLE False pending flights can only be left alone
    ROLLOUT_LOOKAHEAD_S 2880 s minimum span of the forecast
    ROLLOUT_MARGIN_STEPS 20 margin added to the forecast (900 s)
    ROLLOUT_MAX_STEPS 200 upper bound on the forecast
    TIME_SCALE_S 7200 s scale of the window's time features
    MAX_EPISODE_STEPS 200 training cap on episode length, per segment
    MIN_EPISODE_STEPS / HORIZON_STEP_MARGIN 48 / 6 floor and margin of the step limit
    CONTINUING_TASK True the end of the stream is a truncation
    STITCH_SEGMENTS / STITCH_GAP_S 1 / (120, 900) s no stitching by default

    Settings that the published policies change, recorded under windowed_config in artifacts/single_agent/mxp/run_meta.json and artifacts/single_agent/pms/run_meta.json:

    key value described in
    scenario_aircraft_count 20 this page
    stitch_segments 1 stitched streams
    use_case 2 for the PMS policy; absent (use case 1) for the TMB policy the scenario for each procedure
    release_locked, release_dev_s, pay_at_release true, 30.0, true release
    reward_mode, landing_brackets, pbrs_flat_deviation outcome_pbrs, a ladder with a 30 s top bracket, true Objective and reward

    Both also record max_picks: 2 (reselection) and sequence_obs: true (the AMAN-sequence features in the observation). These two are read from environment variables when the code is imported, TADA_MAX_PICKS and TADA_SEQUENCE_OBS, because they size the observation and action spaces. The commands that set them are on Evaluate the published models.
