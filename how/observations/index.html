    Observations¶

    Summary

    At every decision the policy receives a fixed-size dictionary that describes the ten flights in the temporal window: four global features, 17 scalars per aircraft, each aircraft's remaining route relative to its position, its eight most recent clearances, and the masks that mark padding and infeasible choices. Every continuous value is bounded to [-1, 1]. Some features are read from the live world and others from the predicted world, so the policy sees both the current traffic and what will happen if no further clearance is issued. The layout is identical for the trombone (TMB) and point merge (PMS) policies.

    Source: single-agent/models/observations.py (build_global_observation), single-agent/atc_env/windowed_env.py (_build_window_observation, _sequence_state, _add_pick_observation), single-agent/config/config.py, single-agent/config/windowed_config.py.

    Where the values come from¶

    The environment keeps two copies of the simulation. The live world is the traffic as it is now. The predicted world is the result of a no-operation rollout (do-nothing forecast): the live world advanced with no further clearance over a horizon that covers the scheduled landings of the flights in the window. Positions, speeds and routes are read from the live world. Predicted landing-time deviations, predicted infringements and the AMAN-sequence features are read from the predicted world, so they state the consequence of issuing nothing more. The forecast is recomputed after every decision, including a zero-time reselection pick, where it includes the clearance just queued.

    The window, the forecast horizon and the episode rules are described in Episodes and windowing.

    Layout¶

    The observation is a dictionary. A = 10 is the number of slots, C = 15 the number of clearances in the action set (v2 for TMB, v2pms for PMS; both have 15).

    Component Key Shape Content
    Global features global (4,) window time span, traffic load, mean predicted deviation, largest predicted infringement
    Aircraft scalars aircraft (10, 17) kinematics, schedule and conflict forecasts, control status, AMAN-sequence descriptors, same-interval clearance flag
    Relative flight plans flight_plans_rel (10, 20, 11) up to 20 remaining waypoints per flight
    Action histories action_histories (10, 8, 16) the eight most recent clearances per flight
    Flight-plan mask flight_plan_mask (10, 20) rows of flight_plans_rel that hold a real waypoint
    Aircraft-selection mask mask_select (10,) slots that may be selected at this pick
    Clearance mask mask_action_per_ac (10, 15) feasible clearances for each slot
    Reselection mask mask_again (1,) whether another pick may be requested before time advances
    Slot validity mask_aircraft (10,) slots that hold a flight

    Slot i holds the i-th flight of the window in AMAN order. A slot without a flight is all zeros and has mask_aircraft = 0. The policy network applies the same weights to every slot and has no positional input, so the slot index carries no information of its own.

    The scalar width of 17 requires TADA_SEQUENCE_OBS=1 (four AMAN-sequence columns) and TADA_MAX_PICKS=2 (one clearance flag, and the keys mask_select and mask_again). Both published policies were trained with these settings and their run_meta.json records aircraft_scalar_len: 17.

    Coordinate frame¶

    All horizontal positions pass through a translation drawn once per episode, uniformly within ±75 NM on each axis (sample_translation_matrix). Aircraft and waypoints are shifted together, so relative geometry is unchanged, while absolute coordinates differ from episode to episode.

    Encodings¶

    Three maps are used. clip limits a value to the stated range.

    linear, symmetric   sym(v; s)       = clip(v / s, -1, 1)
linear, one-sided   unit(v; lo, hi) = 2 * clip((v - lo) / (hi - lo), 0, 1) - 1
signed logarithm    slog(v; m, f)   = sign(v) * ln(1 + min(|v|, m) / f) / ln(1 + m / f)

    The signed logarithm is used for times. It keeps the sign, saturates at |v| = m, and gives the most resolution near zero: with m = 1800 s and f = 10 s, deviations of 30 s, 60 s and 120 s map to 0.27, 0.37 and 0.49, where a linear map over the same range would give 0.02, 0.03 and 0.07.

    Aircraft scalars¶

    # Name in code Feature Normalisation
    0, 1 pos_x, pos_y horizontal position (NM), translated frame sym(v; 150)
    2 pos_z altitude (ft) sym(v; 45000)
    3 vel_xy ground speed (kt) unit(v; 0, 350)
    4 vz vertical speed (ft/min) sym(v; 3000)
    5, 6 heading_sin, heading_cos sine and cosine of the heading, from the horizontal velocity none (already in [-1, 1])
    7 time_to_target scheduled time to target: AMAN target landing time minus now (s), not below 0 2 * slog(v; 7200, 30) - 1
    8 time_deviation predicted landing-time deviation (s): predicted landing minus target, positive when late slog(v; 1800, 10)
    9 predicted_infringement largest predicted infringement measure of this aircraft, [0, 1] unit(v; 0, 1)
    10 time_to_conflict urgency of its earliest predicted infringement, max(0, 1 - Δt / 1200 s) unit(v; 0, 1)
    11 under_control 1 if the flight is under control, 0 if it has not yet entered the TMA none (0 or 1)
    12 sequence predicted rank displacement: predicted landing rank minus AMAN rank clip(v / 10, -1, 1)
    13 sequence number of order crossings: flights predicted to land on the other side of this one than in the AMAN sequence unit(min(v, 10); 0, 10)
    14 sequence predecessor landing-gap error (s): predicted gap to the AMAN predecessor minus the target gap slog(v; 1800, 10)
    15 sequence successor landing-gap error (s), likewise slog(v; 1800, 10)
    16 pick cleared in the current decision interval none (0 or 1)

    Columns 0–11 are the fields of AircraftScalarFeatures. Columns 12–15 and 16 are appended by the windowed environment.

    Position and speed (0–6) locate the aircraft in the terminal area. The sine and cosine pair encodes the heading without a discontinuity at 360°.

    Scheduled time to target (7) tells the policy how soon the flight is due. It is the schedule, not a prediction.

    Predicted landing-time deviation (8) is what the forecast says will happen if nothing more is done. It is 0 when the forecast lands the flight within the simulator's 10 s tolerance of its target.

    Predicted infringement and time to conflict (9, 10) make a developing interaction visible before it becomes a violation. The measure is taken over the predicted separation events of this aircraft in the next 2880 s:

    measure = min( clip((10 NM - h) / 7 NM, 0, 1), clip((1000 ft - v) / 500 ft, 0, 1) )

    where h and v are the horizontal and vertical separation of the pair. It is 0 at 10 NM or 1000 ft and reaches 1 at the conflict boundary (3 NM and 500 ft). This band is wider than the 3–5 NM band on which the reward prices a predicted infringement (Separation rules): the policy can watch a pair close from 10 NM, before it costs anything. Column 10 is +1 for an event that is imminent and −1 when no event is predicted within 20 minutes. Both columns include events with a partner outside the window.

    Under control (11) separates flights that can receive a clearance from flights that hold a place in the queue but have not yet spawned.

    AMAN-sequence descriptors (12–15) state how far the predicted traffic departs from the fixed AMAN sequence. Landing times are the actual ones for flights already down and the forecast ones for the rest; a flight the forecast does not land is placed at the end of the forecast. A positive rank displacement means the flight is predicted to be overtaken, a negative one that it overtakes. A negative gap error means compression against the neighbouring flight.

    Cleared in the current decision interval (16) is set for an aircraft that already received a clearance at this simulated time. It distinguishes the observation of a second pick from that of the first, and such an aircraft can only be left alone until time advances.

    Features defined on the window¶

    No input depends on how many flights the stream contains. A 20-flight training stream is treated as an excerpt of continuous traffic, and the same policy can be run on a longer stream without any feature leaving its range. The following features are therefore defined on the window:

    Feature Definition Encoding
    scalar 7, scheduled time to target AMAN target landing time minus now signed logarithm over 7200 s with a 30 s knee, mapped to [-1, 1]
    global[0], window time span scheduled time until the last flight in the window is due the same
    global[1], traffic load number of flights in the window that are under control unit(n; 0, 10)
    global[2], mean deviation mean absolute predicted landing-time deviation over the flights in the window 2 * slog(v; 1800, 10) - 1
    global[3], largest infringement maximum of scalar 9 over the flights in the window as scalar 9

    The 7200 s scale (WindowedConfig.TIME_SCALE_S) is chosen to cover the time span of ten consecutive flights.

    A flight that has not yet spawned may occupy a slot. Its state and route are visible, its under_control flag is 0, and its row of the clearance mask allows only do nothing. An active aircraft more than ten places behind the head of the queue is not represented at all; this is the partial observability that temporal windowing accepts.

    Relative flight plans¶

    For each flight, the first 20 points of its remaining route, in flying order. Rows beyond the end of the route are zero and are marked in flight_plan_mask.

    # Feature Normalisation
    0, 1 waypoint position x, y (NM), translated frame sym(v; 150)
    2 waypoint altitude (ft): the mean of its minimum and maximum altitude constraints, or the aircraft's altitude when it has none sym(v; 45000)
    3, 4 offset from the aircraft to the waypoint, x and y (NM) sym(v; 150)
    5 altitude offset (ft) sym(v; 40000)
    6 horizontal distance from the aircraft (NM) unit(v; 0, 150)
    7, 8 sine and cosine of the bearing from the aircraft to the waypoint none
    9 target speed at the waypoint (kt); 0 when the waypoint has none unit(v; 0, 350)
    10 target speed available none (0 or 1)

    The remaining route determines which path modifications are still possible. A clearance that changes the route (a skip, a trombone extension, a vector to the ILS, a direct to the merge point) appears here once it has been executed.

    Action histories¶

    For each flight, its eight most recent picks, most recent first (Config.MAX_HISTORY = 8). A record has 16 values: a validity flag followed by a one-hot vector over the 15 clearances. Unused records are all zeros. Both action sets have 15 clearances, so the record width is 16 for the TMB and the PMS policy alike. A do nothing pick addressed to the aircraft is recorded like any other clearance. Records carry no timestamp.

    The history lets the policy distinguish natural motion from the lasting effect of an earlier clearance, for example a speed that is low because the aircraft was slowed down.

    Global features¶

    # Feature Normalisation
    0 scheduled time until the last flight in the window is due (s) 2 * slog(v; 7200, 30) - 1
    1 number of flights in the window that are under control unit(n; 0, 10)
    2 mean absolute predicted landing-time deviation over the window (s) 2 * slog(v; 1800, 10) - 1
    3 largest predicted infringement measure in the window as aircraft scalar 9

    Masks¶

    Flight-plan mask (flight_plan_mask). 1 for a row of flight_plans_rel that holds a real waypoint. The route encoder averages over these rows only.

    Slot validity (mask_aircraft). 1 for a slot that holds a flight. The network uses it to keep empty slots out of the self-attention and out of the pooled context.

    Aircraft-selection mask (mask_select). The slots the aircraft head may pick: flights under control that have not been cleared in the current decision interval. If no flight in the window is under control, every occupied slot is selectable, so the mask is never empty.

    Clearance mask (mask_action_per_ac). For each slot, the clearances that are physically and procedurally feasible for that flight now, computed from aircraft limits, waypoint constraints and route geometry (single-agent/actions/; see Actions). The environment then reduces the row to do nothing for a flight that is not under control, has been released, or has already been cleared in this interval. Do nothing is removed for an aircraft whose forecast contains a conflict (scalar 9 at its maximum), so that the policy must act on it. A row is never empty.

    Reselection mask (mask_again). 1 if the policy may ask for another pick before simulated time advances: the current pick is the first of the interval (the budget is two clearances per 45 s) and at least one other selectable aircraft would remain. See Reselection.

    What the observation does not contain¶

    • Aircraft beyond the window: a flight more than ten places behind the head of the queue.
    • The time at which an earlier clearance was issued: the action history is ordered, not timestamped.
    • The effect of future clearances: every predicted feature assumes that nothing more is issued. Rollout search is the mechanism that evaluates sequences of clearances.
