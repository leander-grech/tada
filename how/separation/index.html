    Separation rules¶

    Summary

    Two aircraft are in an infringement when they are closer than 5 NM and 1000 ft, and in a conflict (a loss of separation) when they are closer than 3 NM and 500 ft. A conflict that actually happens in the live world ends the episode. A conflict or infringement that is only predicted by the do-nothing forecast ends nothing: it is graded by a severity between 0 and 1 and fed to the reward shaping, the observation and the masks, so that the agent has a reason to resolve it before it happens.

    Source: single-agent/utils/infringement_utils.py, single-agent/actions/rewards.py, single-agent/atc_env/single_agent_env.py, constants in single-agent/config/config.py.

    Two thresholds¶

    horizontal separation vertical separation
    infringement closer than 5 NM and closer than 1000 ft
    conflict = loss of separation closer than 3 NM and closer than 500 ft

    Both conditions must hold at the same moment. Two aircraft 2 NM apart horizontally but 1000 ft apart vertically are legally separated. A conflict is the severe case of an infringement: every conflict is also an infringement.

    Events are counted per pair of aircraft, not per time step. A pair contributes at most one infringement and at most one conflict to an episode, however long the encounter lasts. Only arrivals in the scenario are considered.

    Detection¶

    Detection happens in two layers.

    The simulator records proximity events. At each simulation step it checks every pair of active aircraft that are under control. When a pair is close enough, it records an event with the two callsigns, the time, and the horizontal and vertical separation. In the Python interface these events have the type Infringement.LOSS_OF_SEPARATION. The name is wider than the meaning: with the published settings an event is recorded for pairs out to 10 NM horizontally, twice the 5 NM safe-zone radius that the environment passes to the simulator (Config.MISSION_SAFEZONE_RADIUS_NM). An event is a record that two aircraft were near each other, not yet a finding that they were too near.

    The environment grades each event. From the two separations stored in an event it computes:

    • a severity, which is zero outside the infringement thresholds and reaches one at the conflict thresholds. Severity is what the reward charges and what termination tests;
    • a proximity, the same construction on a wider horizontal band that starts at 10 NM instead of 5 NM. Proximity is what the policy observes, so that it can watch an encounter develop before it costs anything (Observations).

    The same grading is applied to events in the live world and to events in the predicted world.

    Severity¶

    Severity combines a horizontal ramp and a vertical ramp. With h the horizontal separation in NM and v the vertical separation in ft:

    ramp_h   = clamp( (5 − h) / (5 − 3),          0, 1 )
ramp_v   = clamp( (1000 − v) / (1000 − 500),  0, 1 )
severity = min(ramp_h, ramp_v) ²
    • Severity is 0 when h ≥ 5 NM or v ≥ 1000 ft: no infringement.
    • Severity is 1 when h ≤ 3 NM and v ≤ 500 ft: a conflict.
    • In between, the pair is in an infringement that is not a conflict, and severity says how deep.

    The minimum of the two ramps expresses "both must be small": a pair is only as severe as its better-separated axis allows. The square makes the measure convex. It is steepest just outside the conflict boundary, where a small gain in separation matters most, and it flattens to zero slope at the infringement boundary, so there is no kink for the policy to sit against at legal spacing.

    The figure shows severity over both separations, computed with the code's pair_severity. The fully coloured corner is a conflict.

    The function is pair_severity in single-agent/utils/infringement_utils.py; proximity is pair_proximity in the same file. In the evaluation records, a pair whose severity lies strictly between 0 and 1 is counted as an infringement (warning_pairs in single-agent/analysis/evaluation_records.py), and a pair that reaches 1 is a conflict.

    Realised and predicted¶

    The grading is the same in both worlds, but the consequences are not.

    where it is read what it drives
    realised events of the live world: things that happened termination of the episode, the conflict penalty in the reward, whether the episode is solved
    predicted events of the predicted world: the no-operation rollout from the current state the separation term of the reward shaping, the conflict features of the observation, one clearance mask, the release rule

    Realised. After every 45 s step the environment scans the events accumulated in the live world. If any pair has reached severity 1, a conflict has occurred. Nothing predicted can trigger this, however imminent.

    Predicted. The no-operation rollout produces the events that will occur if no further clearance is given. They are used in four places:

    • Reward shaping. For each pair, the earliest predicted event is taken, with its severity and its time-to-infringement. Pairs with at least one aircraft in the window contribute to the separation potential, weighted by severity and discounted by how far in the future the event lies. A clearance that removes a predicted infringement, or pushes it further away, is rewarded at once; one that creates an infringement is penalised at once. The exact form is on Objective and reward.
    • Observation. Each aircraft carries its largest predicted proximity and the time to its earliest predicted event (Observations).
    • Masking. An aircraft that the forecast puts in a conflict within the next 2880 s cannot be given DO_NOTHING (Actions and reselection).
    • Release. A flight is released from the window only if it appears in no predicted proximity event (Episodes and temporal windowing).

    Pricing predicted events is what gives the agent foresight. If only realised events counted, the agent would receive no signal about a developing encounter until the aircraft were already inside the infringement thresholds, which is too late to act.

    What ends an episode¶

    A realised conflict ends the episode immediately. The step that causes it receives a fixed penalty (100 with the published settings), the episode is reported with end_reason = "separation", and it is not solved. The penalty is larger than the largest improvement in landing reward available to the ten flights in the window, so no gain in punctuality can pay for a conflict (Objective and reward).

    An infringement that is not a conflict does not end the episode and does not by itself prevent it from being solved. It is charged through the severity while it is predicted, and it is counted in the evaluation records.

    An aircraft that leaves the airspace ends the episode in the same way and with the same penalty (end_reason = "airspace_exit").

    The other ways an episode can end, every flight landed or the step limit reached, are described on Episodes and temporal windowing.

    Constants¶

    All in Config (single-agent/config/config.py):

    constant value role
    SEVERITY_SAFEZONE_NM 5.0 horizontal infringement threshold; severity reaches 0 here
    SEVERITY_CONFLICT_NM 3.0 horizontal conflict threshold; severity reaches 1 here
    VERTICAL_SAFEZONE_RADIUS_FT 1000.0 vertical infringement threshold
    VERTICAL_CONFLICT_RADIUS_FT 500.0 vertical conflict threshold
    SEVERITY_SHAPE_P 2.0 exponent applied to the combined ramp
    LOS_SEVERITY_THRESHOLD 1.0 severity at which a realised event is a conflict
    DETECTION_SAFEZONE_RADIUS_NM 10.0 outer edge of the proximity band used by the observation
    DETECTION_CONFLICT_RADIUS_NM 3.0 proximity reaches 1 here
    MISSION_SAFEZONE_RADIUS_NM 5.0 safe-zone radius passed to the simulator (with TADA_GENERATOR=procedure)
